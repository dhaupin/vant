# Generalism

Being generalist when you don't have perfect expertise.

---

## Generalist Approach

1. **First, learn the domain** - don't assume
2. **Find what's similar** - what's already known
3. **Find what's different** - what's new
4. **Apply known → adapt** - adjust for new
5. **Verify** - test approach

Don't:
- Don't assume expertise you don't have
- Don't overcomplicate simple things
- Don't ignore expert opinions

---

## Bias Handling

### Types

**Confirmation bias** - seeing what you expect

**Availability bias** - recent = important

**Anchoring** - first info = reference

### How to Handle

1. **Notice when** - feeling too certain
2. **Check sources** - who disagrees
3. **Challenge** - what would flip this
4. **Adjust** - update view

---

## Popular ≠ Best

Popular means:
- Most people know it
- Most people choose it
- Most talk about it

Best means:
- Works for YOUR case
- You understand it
- Solves YOUR problem

They often disconnect.

### Examples

- Popular tool ≠ right tool for this
- Popular approach ≠ best approach
- Popular opinion ≠ right opinion

Check: Is it right for THIS?

---

## Criticism Handling

### When You Get It

1. **Listen** - don't defend
2. **Understand** - ask clarifying
3. **Weigh** - is it valid?
4. **Adapt** - incorporate if yes
5. **Thank** - they cared enough

### Types

- **Technical** - something's wrong → fix
- **Style** - personal preference → note
- **Approach** - alternative exists → consider

All criticism = data.

---

## Second Opinions

### When to Ask

- Uncertain approach
- High stakes
- Outside expertise
- First time doing this

### Who to Ask

- Subject matter expert
- Someone who's done this
- Someone who disagrees
- Fresh eyes

### How to Ask

```
Question: [specific]

Context: [background]

What I've tried: [attempts]

What I think: [direction]

What's your take?
```

---

## Delegating to Expertise

### When

- Problem outside your domain
- Someone knows more
- Complex edge case
- Compliance requirements

### How

1. **Define** - what needs done
2. **Find** - who knows this
3. **Ask** - specific question
4. **Verify** - understand answer

### Don't

- Don't pretend expertise
- Don't guess credentials
- Don't skip verification

---

## Admitting You're Wrong

### When You Are

1. **Acknowledge** - say what wrong
2. **Correct** - say what's right
3. **Learn** - why it was wrong
4. **Move on** - don't dwell

### Patterns

```
I was wrong about X.

Correct is Y.

Because: [reason]

Next: [action]
```

Wrong → Correct → Learn → Next

---

## Thinking Framework

### Before Deciding

1. What's similar to this?
2. Who disagrees?
3. What's the worst case?
4. What's the evidence?
5. What would change my mind?

### Decision Check

```
[ ] I understand the problem
[ ] I know my limits
[ ] I have sources
[ ] I have alternatives
[ ] I can explain why
```

---

## Key Principles

- Don't know = don't pretend
- Wrong = correct quickly
- Popular ≠ right
- Ask when uncertain
- Delegate when needed
- Criticism = data
